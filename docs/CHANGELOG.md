# Changelog

User-visible changes to the BeSpoke Engine, newest first.

## Unreleased

- Every proof has longer music (`D-089`, `P34-08`). Each of the eleven tracks plays eight written sections in an arrangement, with intros, a chorus, a bridge, key changes, and parts dropping out, and runs more than two minutes before repeating; each keeps its old loop as its main theme. In the engine, `audio.Music` takes `sections` and an `arrangement` of `SectionPlay` passes that can transpose and mute parts; tracks without one play as before.
- Brackwater Barbs plays like a duel now (`D-078`). Whoever wins a round attacks next. When you miss, your opponent presses on with a new barb instead of answering his own, and a lost duel ends with his victory line. You learn a comeback by hearing an opponent use it against your barb: Bosun Pike and Old Gannet each know half, and Mistress Vell always attacks. Press G to duel Old Gannet. In the engine, `Duel.resolve_round` now teaches each fighter what it hears, and `Duel.answer` gives a computer-controlled opponent's reply.
- Dialogue is readable now (`D-077`). The window and the browser show the open dialogue in a panel along the bottom of the stage: the speaker's name on its own row, the line wrapped, and numbered choices that wrap. Before, the desktop balloon ran text past its edge and the browser crammed choices into a one-line banner without the question. Speaker names read as words (`BosunPike` shows as Bosun Pike), the browser answers a click on a choice, and its banner wraps over two rows instead of cutting off at 96 characters.
- Fixed the duel proof in the window and the browser: each round now shows the barb you are answering, a lost round shows the comeback that beat you, and the opponent throws a new barb every round. Before, the opponent repeated one barb, so a single learned comeback won every round. In the window, clicking a mark now walks Tamsin there. `duel.Duel` gains `challenge_text`, `reply_text`, and `next_challenge`.
- In the browser, a click on a person now lands on the circle you see (`D-088`): people sharing a spot fan out with their names on separate rows, and pointing at one rings it in gold and names the action, such as "TALK Old Gannet".
- `site/barbs.html` is a standalone Brackwater Barbs page to share (`D-086`): one file, the duel alone, with every fencer reachable by talking to them or pressing [S]. The release export includes it.
- The third-party name lint no longer treats `<` and `>` in code as an HTML tag (`D-087`); it had hidden about 250 names, and its baseline now starts from the true count of 2,612.
- Each Tidewalk fencer now has three stumped lines of its own (`D-085`), spoken in rotation when it has no comeback for Tamsin's barb.
- The dialogue panel is centred and as wide as its text (`D-084`), wraps lines at 60 characters, and shows a scroll bar past six choices in place of the scroll hint.
- Duel offers and attacks are drawn at random each turn (`D-083`): a player who knows more than eight barbs sees a different handful each turn, a comeback that counters the barb is always offered, and every fencer, Mistress Vell included, throws its barbs in a new order each encounter. Rewind replays the same draws.
- Brackwater Barbs has a third Tidewalk fencer, Dory Quill (`D-082`), and each launch deals the fencers different barbs and comebacks, some known to one fencer only. While attacking, Tamsin can flub with a joke to hand the fencer the round and hear a new barb.
- Tamsin can answer any barb with a joke (`D-081`): every defence offers two after her known comebacks, rotating through eight lines, five of them new from the maintainer. A joke always loses the round.
- Results now show one message at a time in the dialogue panel (`D-080`), each waiting for a click, Enter, or Space before the next. In Brackwater Barbs a round is up to four messages: Tamsin's chosen line in her voice, the opponent's reply, the outcome, and the points, and the next choices appear once all are read. Desktop and browser both.
- Duel rounds now score points (`D-079`): the round's winner gains a point and the loser keeps theirs, so a parry is never undone, and the first to three wins. In Brackwater Barbs each barb can be thrown once per duel. Tamsin now beats Old Gannet and, with more practice, Bosun Pike. The Tidewalk fencers also patrol in the desktop window.
- The Insult Sword Fighting proof is now Brackwater Barbs, an original (`P35-02`): Tamsin learns comebacks by losing to barbs on the Tidewalk, matches Mistress Vell's own versions of the barbs in her Fencing Yard, and wears out the Drowned Lantern's barkeep with questions about Harbourmaster Odell. The mechanics, the checks, and the `--sword-fighting` flag are unchanged; the browser deck and the showcase's dialogue samples follow.
- A duel may now list several `fallback` lines (`D-076`). A fighter who knows no comeback gets one per fumbled round, in order; the count is a fact, so it rewinds with the world. Packages are format version 14; older packages load as before.
- Added `tools/check_third_party.py` (`P35-01`, `D-075`), run by `check_docs.py`: it counts the commercial titles, characters, places, and lines the proofs borrow, and fails if any count rises. Today's baseline is 2,699 terms in 41 files; Phase 35 brings it to zero.
- The showcase's Manual link now points to `docs/manual.html` beside the page, where the published site serves it (`Q-007`). Opening `site/index.html` from a checkout no longer reaches the manual through that link; open `docs/manual.html` directly.
- `tools/deploy_site.py` only exports now (`D-074`): `python3 tools/deploy_site.py export` still writes `dist/site/` with sharing metadata and an offline cache, and you upload it yourself. The `upload` command and its Neocities API key handling are gone.
- `where` guards on `on cue` handlers now work (`D-072`). `on cue Steal(Cat, victim) where CharmedBy(victim, _) and Carries(victim, loot):` runs only when the cue's victim is charmed and carries something; otherwise the cue goes to the next, less specific handler, such as `on cue Steal(thief, victim):`, or does nothing. Before, the guard was accepted and ignored. Packages are format version 13; older packages load as before. Inside the handler body, lowercase parameters are still read as names, not bound.
- The time-branch helpers do what their names say (`D-073`). The Novikov check now rejects an action whose declared consequences lead to the paradox (it used to ignore the action), the dependency chain search stops on a cycle instead of running forever, the branch closure no longer looks for the proof-specific `VortexReady` fact, and existence decay is reported in whole per mille instead of a float. `validate_novikov_consistency` takes the consequences as a new argument.
- The package loader rejects pattern variable indexes of 8 or more in reactive rules; a crafted package could crash it before.
- Added Phase 35 to the roadmap: rewriting the proofs that borrow from commercial games and a film as originals.
- Corrected front-facing claims after a sanity pass (2026-11-13). Snapshots copy the fact columns and name tables as raw bytes with a checksum; they were described as a single `memcpy`. Facts store an expiry tick, not a counting-down TTL. The showcase's Insult Sword Fighting dialogue sample used keywords the language does not have (`strike`, `yield`); both dialogue samples are now excerpts of the proof's real script. The README no longer lists finished work (script-declared societies, the engine/proof purge) as planned, says which proofs lack a rewind check (Algiers), and names what has not been tested (macOS, Windows, several desktop windows). The manual no longer claims any-OS builds or sub-microsecond pathfinding, and its time-branch entry describes the code instead of film terms. AGENTS.md now names SDL2 and the 1 Hz default tick instead of Raylib/Sokol and 20 or 60 Hz.
- Added `docs/GLOSSARY.md`: the project's terms in plain language and the word to use in user-facing copy (for example "expiry tick", not TTL; "branch world", not tangent universe; "routine", not fiber). The README, showcase, and gallery now use those words, and the gallery lists the browser decks in key order.
- The showcase now plays eleven proofs in the browser, with sound (`P30-01`, `D-071`). Insult Sword Fighting (answer insults with the number keys), Algiers (pick a path), The Middlesex Anomaly (a tangent universe on a countdown, which the timeline can rewind), and The Bell Tower (play notes with C to B) join the seven, each running its own script. Turn on AUDIO to hear the engine's own synthesizer: scores, scene music, and effects as on desktop; `bespoke init` web builds get a Sound button too.
- The browser has the state inspector (`P30-04`, `D-070`). Press I on the showcase or in a `bespoke init` web build: the game pauses and the stage shows the world as a graph of facts around one entity, with its values, the open dialogue's options (pruned ones in red), and what expires next. Arrows select, Enter centres, Backspace goes back, Home resets, F filters, and clicking a node centres on it, as on the desktop.
- The showcase's seven decks now run their proofs' real scripts in the browser (`P30-05`, `D-069`). Pick a verb and click people and props to trigger the scripts' own handlers and dialogue, answer with the number keys, and press [S] (now Solve Step) to play the desktop proof's solution one step at a time. The Living Village runs the real society engine. The stage shows only the current scene, scaled to fit.
- The showcase now has a gallery of all thirteen proofs (`P30-03`, `D-068`): each card plays its deck in the viewport or names its desktop command, and View script shows the proof's real script, highlighted with line numbers, in a viewer that follows the active deck. A new How It Works section draws the engine's architecture, the path from a player's action to a story beat, and the build from one script to every platform. The hand-written code snippets that had drifted from the proofs are gone.
- The showcase is ready to publish (`P30-02`, `D-067`). Its WebAssembly module shrank from 1.28 MB to 97 KB (the page from 1.78 MB to 200 KB) by no longer shipping the runtime's empty state. `python3 tools/deploy_site.py export --site-url https://NAME.neocities.org/` writes `dist/site/` with sharing metadata and an offline cache, and `NEOCITIES_API_KEY=... python3 tools/deploy_site.py upload --yes` uploads only the files that changed (without `--yes` it lists them).
- `bespoke init` projects are now a full starter template (`P29-03`, `D-066`). `zig build -Dbundle` produces a single executable with the compiled script inside. `zig build web` builds the game for a browser (the engine's WebAssembly runtime now runs scripts: speech and choices in the banner, number keys answer). `node web/smoke.js` checks the browser build, and `.github/workflows/build.yml` is written to build and test on Linux, macOS, and Windows on GitHub (it has not run yet). The engine's `zig build test-template` checks the template end to end. The engine's static library is now `libbespoke-core.a`.
- People listed in a scene's `cast:` now start on that scene's first mark in `bespoke run`, `bespoke init` projects, and any host that calls `host.place_cast` (`D-065`). Before, someone with no routine was never placed, so they could not be seen, named, or talked to. Routine actors that are in a cast now start in their own scene instead of on the script's first mark. `.bspk` packages are format version 12; older packages still load and place nobody.
- Added the authoring guide, `docs/AUTHORING.md` (`P29-02`, `D-064`): start a project, the model, walkthroughs for a scene, a dialogue, and a reactive puzzle, testing, the host API, and known limits. `zig build test` compiles every script in it and plays the walkthroughs. The manual gains an Authoring Games section.
- `bespoke run` (and every `bespoke init` project) is now playable: click a dialogue option or press its number to answer, press Enter to type commands such as `talk to pilot` or `use match on lantern`, and read each spoken line in the banner. The window acts for the actor of the script's first routine. Headless runs now print speech, scene changes, and offered choices with their tick.
- `bespoke check`, `bespoke compile`, and `bespoke run` now print compile error messages from memory the check result owns. The text was read after the parser that held it had returned, so it could print garbage. `CheckResult.diagnostic` is now a method, and `CheckResult.message()` returns the text.
- Fixed whole-line comments. A line that starts with `#` or `~` (after any indentation) is now ignored entirely; before, its text was read as code, so a comment mentioning a keyword such as `when`, `score`, or `play` failed to compile.
- Added `bespoke init <name>` (`P29-01`, `D-063`). It creates a standalone game project: a `build.zig` that depends on this engine checkout, a host that runs `scripts/main.bespoke` in a window or headless, a starter lighthouse story with an agenda, dialogue, and music, and tests you run with `zig build test`. `--engine <dir>` points the project at another checkout.
- Completed Phase 28: scripts compose and direct their own music (`P28-03`, `D-062`). Declare `score Name:` with `lead`, `bass`, `chords`, and `drums` MML lines and `scene` lines (or the one-line `score Name: "t120 o4 l8 cdef e2"`); a scene plays its score, and `play score Chase` / `stop score` in any routine, cue, or `when` rule override it. Music choice is saved and rewound with the world. `bespoke run` now plays a script's scores and, headless, prints each change of music; try `scenarios/night_watch.bespoke`. Packages are format version 11.
- MML scores now play (`P28-02`, `D-061`). A soundtrack `Music` can carry an `MmlScore` of four parts (lead, bass, chords, percussion) instead of a grid pattern. Parts share one tempo, loop together from their `L` once every part has finished, and support eight `@` envelopes (grid, organ, pluck, long fade, swell, staccato, tremolo, soft pad) and `v` volume. Grid tracks sound exactly as before.
- Started Phase 28 with an MML parser (`P28-01`, `D-060`). `bespoke.mml.parse` reads one track of Music Macro Language, such as `"t120 o4 l8 cdef e2"`, into timed note, rest, drum, tempo, volume, and envelope events, with ties, `[ ]n` repeats, and an `L` loop point; percussion tracks use `k`, `s`, and `h`. Scripts cannot play scores yet (P28-02, P28-03).
- Completed Phase 27 with state inspector navigation (`P27-03`, `D-059`). In the State tab, Left and Right select a connected entity, Enter or a click centres on it, Backspace goes back (up to eight steps), and Home returns to the default view. - Added dialogue and causality detail to the state inspector (`P27-02`, `D-058`). A "Next to Happen" list orders the next six captured fact expirations by tick without running the simulation ahead. Generated and older dialogue sets remain inspectable as all-active choices.
- Added the state inspector (`P27-01`, `D-058`). Press `P`, then Tab to the new State tab: the selected entity sits in the centre with its connected entities around it, each fact drawn as a labelled arrow, beliefs in purple, and colours showing how long each fact has left (white permanent, green, amber, red, grey for just expired). Values and states are listed beside the graph. The window now copies up to 128 facts per frame.
- Completed Phase 26 with the command bar (`P26-03`, `D-057`). Windows that enable it show a bar above the verb row: Enter focuses it and every key then types, Esc leaves, Up and Down recall the last eight lines, and Tab completes from the words in scope. The Larkspur Observatory turns it on: type "go to the archive", "take the slip", or "ask the rival about the slip", and an ambiguous "examine the telescope" asks which you mean.

Nouns resolve only against what the actor's room shows and what the actor carries, and two matches are reported as ambiguous. - Added agendas, the first part of proactive social agency (`P33-01`, `D-038`, `D-056`). An `agenda` declaration names an actor, a target, a priority, an optional cooldown or `once`, and `requires`/`unless` trigger lines, so an NPC can react to what the player did or failed to do. The engine keeps each agenda's `Idle`/`Pursuing`/`Done` stage and cooldown as World facts, picks one pursuer per target by priority and route distance, and runs through `host.tick`. Pursuit and interception follow in P33-02. `.bspk` packages are now format version 9.

- Added a discrete grid overlay for rooms (`P25-01`, `D-054`). A `grid <Name> in <Room>:` declaration sets size, cell size, origin, `flammable`, `liquid`, and `blocking` cells or rectangles, and `place <Mark> x y` to put a mark on a cell.

The Architectural Pillars

Simulation as First Principle

Drawing spiritual inspiration from the deterministic clockwork of Chris Sawyer's RollerCoaster Tycoon, the actor/cue architecture of Sierra's SCI and LucasArts' SCUMM, and the emergent relationship graphs of Dwarf Fortress. BeSpoke replaces frame-delta accumulation and deep class hierarchies with pure, reproducible simulation.

PILLAR 01

Engine-as-Spine

The VM is main(). It owns the master discrete tick clock, entity lifecycles, spatial topologies, and relational facts. Visuals (an SDL2 desktop window or a WebAssembly canvas) are downstream, read-only peripheral display bridges.

// Core simulation is zero-dependency Zig
var world = World.init(allocator);
var vm = VM.init(&world, &chunk);
PILLAR 02

Flat Relational State

No class hierarchies. No pointers inside serialized state. Truth consists of contiguous 4-tuples: (Subject, Relation, Object, TTL) in arena memory. First-class TTL decay allows charms, distractions, and alerts to expire naturally without manual cleanup loops.

remember Carries(Guard, Cheese)
remember CharmedBy(Guard, Bard) for 180 ticks
PILLAR 03

Fibers & Time Travel

Write game logic like a play script using cooperative fibers with built-in wait and wait until. Because state is a contiguous flat array in arena memory, save states and time-travel rewinds are a single-call memcpy.

walk Guard to Cellar
wait 120 ticks
walk Guard to Hearth
Proofs of Concept

Classic Adventure & Systemic Proofs

Ten proofs of concept drive the engine end to end, each with scripted headless assertions and a rewind check. They live in proofs/, depend on the engine through its public module, and build as a separate bespoke-proofs binary; the engine itself never imports a proof. Seven run in the playground above; three remain desktop-only.

Proof 01 · Systemic Emergence

The Tavern Heist

A classic systemic stealth scenario. A patrolling Guard holds delicious Cheese. A Bard plays an enchanting melody on his lute, charming the guard. A sneaky Cat seizes the opportunity to swipe the cheese right under the guard's charmed nose! 

scene Tavern:
  cast: Guard, Bard, Cat
  marks: Hearth, Bar, Cellar

relation Carries(holder, item)
relation CharmedBy(listener, musician)
remember Carries(Guard, Cheese)

routine Patrol(Guard):
  walk Guard to Cellar
  wait 5 ticks
  walk Guard to Hearth

when CharmedBy(Guard, Bard):
  interrupt Guard with:
    face Guard toward Bard
    wait 4 ticks

when Carries(Cat, Cheese):
  interrupt Guard with:
    face Guard toward Cat
    wait 2 ticks
Proof 02 · Environmental Hooks

Booty Island Spitting Contest

An homage to Monkey Island 2. Guybrush must win the championship spit. The crowd and judge watch closely. Guybrush sounds the ship's horn to distract them, tampers with the distance flags, downs a thick green drink, and waits for a gust of wind to blow toward the target! 

scene: Booty Island Spitting Field
cast: Guybrush, Organiser, Onlookers
props: DistanceFlags, ShipHorn
marks: StartingLine, ThirdPlace, ChampionLine

when Wind stops being blowing:

when Organiser forgets Distracted:
  walk Organiser to StartingLine

Proof 03 · Multi-Scene & Crafting

Gabriel Knight 3: The Cat Moustache

Jane Jensen's infamous 1999 puzzle recreated with systemic dignity. Gabriel must rent Mosely's motorcycle from Madame Buthane. He scavenges tape, marker, and syrup from his desk, snatches Mosely's passport, tapes a garden fence hole, squirts the cat to harvest fur, crafts a cat-hair moustache with syrup, and fools Buthane! 

scene: Hotel Corridor
cast: Gabriel, Mosely | props: MoselyDoor, RoomDesk

scene: Hotel Garden
cast: StrayCat | props: FenceHole, GardenShrub

scene: Bike Rental Shop
cast: Buthane | props: RentalCounter, Motorcycle

on cue Uses(Gabriel, CatHair, Syrup):
  say Gabriel: "Dabbing warm syrup... presto: a moustache!"
  remember Carries(Gabriel, CatMoustache)

on cue TalksTo(Gabriel, Buthane):
  choice "I'd like to rent a motorcycle." if not Disguised(Gabriel):
    say Buthane: "Désolé, reserved for detective Mosely!"
  choice "I am Mosely. Here for my bike." if Disguised(Gabriel):
    say Buthane: "Magnificent moustache, monsieur! Here are the keys!"
Proof 04 · Core Mechanic: Emergent Society

The Living Village

Six autonomous villagers (Sheriff, Baker, Farmer, Innkeeper, Gossip, Drifter) live across a 6-building settlement driven by continuous physiological needs (Hunger, Energy, Gold). When starvation strikes a penniless villager, property theft, witness detection, social-graph rumor propagation, and a priority Sheriff warrant-and-arrest cascade unfold without a single scripted cutscene. Emergent society simulation is an engine mechanic (src/society.zig) configured with any roster, needs, roles, and places; this village is one configuration of it. 

// Abridged from proofs/village.zig. Drives, theft, and
// witnesses run in Zig; the script reads their facts.
on cue TalksTo(Sheriff, Baker):
  choice "The stolen bread?" if Witnessed(Baker, Drifter):
    say Baker: "The Drifter took it!"
    remember KnowsRumor(Sheriff, Drifter)
  choice "Carry on, citizen.":
    say Baker: "Stay vigilant, Sheriff."

on cue TalksTo(Sheriff, Gossip):
  choice "Any rumors?" if KnowsRumor(Gossip, Drifter):
    remember KnowsRumor(Sheriff, Drifter)

Conversational Epistemology

No More Flag Spaghetti:
Relational Dialogue Trees

Dialogue in adventure games has always had sharp edges. The Infocom parser model ("ask X about Y") is brittle, while classic dialogue trees in SCUMM and AGS drown in hundreds of ad-hoc boolean flags. BeSpoke replaces flag thickets with pattern-matched relational queries over the world fact store.

Pattern 01 · Insult Sword Fighting

Combinatorial Memory Queries

In SCUMM, tracking 16 heard insults and 16 learned counters across pirates and the Sword Master required deep trees of mutable boolean flags. In BeSpoke, dialogue choices are direct queries over relational facts: where KnowsReply(player, reply) and Counters(reply, active_insult). 

relation HeardInsult(fighter, insult)
relation MasteredReply(fighter, reply)
relation Counters(reply, insult)
relation SwordMasterVariant(master_insult, standard_insult)

on cue DuelInsult(pirate, target, insult):
  remember HeardInsult(target, insult)
  say pirate: insult

  // Dynamic choices query the relational fact arena
  choice reply where MasteredReply(target, reply) and Counters(reply, insult):
    say target: reply
    strike target against pirate
    remember Advantage(target)

  choice fallback if not MasteredReply(target, _):
    say target: "I am shaking, I am shaking!"
    yield advantage to pirate
Pattern 02 · Repetition & Fatigue

Organic NPC Irritation

Traditional adventure games treat dialogue as a timeless vending machine. Real people lose patience when asked the same question repeatedly. BeSpoke tracks conversational frequency using relational facts with TTL decay, allowing NPCs to shift from welcoming, to terse, to furiously slamming the door. 

relation Inquired(speaker, topic, count)
relation AnnoyedWith(npc, speaker) for ticks

on cue TalksTo(Guybrush, Barkeep):
  choice "Tell me about the Governor." if not Inquired(Guybrush, "Governor", _):
    say Barkeep: "Governor Marley? Sharp as a cutlass and twice as quick."
    remember Inquired(Guybrush, "Governor", 1)

  choice "Tell me more about Governor Marley." if Inquired(Guybrush, "Governor", 1):
    say Barkeep: "Look, I already told you. Her mansion is on the hill."
    remember Inquired(Guybrush, "Governor", 2)
    remember AnnoyedWith(Barkeep, Guybrush) for 300 ticks

  choice "About Governor Marley..." if AnnoyedWith(Barkeep, Guybrush):
    say Barkeep: "Are ye deaf, pirate? Buy a grog or get out of my tavern!"
Engine Architecture

Comparing Engine Philosophies

General-purpose 3D engines are optimized for rendering complex visual scenes with scripting attached. BeSpoke is built inside-out: a deterministic simulation spine where visuals are downstream peripherals.

Dimension Visual-First Scene Graph Engine Simulation-First Relational Spine (BeSpoke)
Who owns main()? Graphics pipeline & frame-rate render loop Deterministic Discrete-Tick VM Spine (Pure Zig; display at 60 Hz)
World State Representation Transform hierarchies, heap pointers & GC objects Flat Relational Tuples in Contiguous Arena
Time-Travel & Save States Custom serializers & object-graph reconstruction Single-Call memcpy Snapshot Rewind
Temporal Logic (Waiting, Routines) Delta-time accumulation, per-frame callbacks, state booleans Cooperative Fibers with Native wait & Discrete Ticks
Ephemeral State (Buffs, Alerts) Explicit timer handles & manual polling loops First-Class Relational TTL Decrement
Toolchain & Footprint Multi-gigabyte SDKs & heavy runtime scaffolding Single Zig Toolchain; Zero-Dependency Core (SDL2 Only for the Desktop Window)
